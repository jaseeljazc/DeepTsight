# Executive summary

DeepTsight requires a high-performing B2B consulting website that positions the business as a premium, trusted authority in control systems, electrical and instrumentation engineering, plant reliability and operational technology cybersecurity. The site should operate as both a credibility platform and a lead-generation channel for enterprise and critical-infrastructure stakeholders.

The experience must feel technically rigorous, calm and assured. Information should be easy to scan, claims should be evidence-led and the interface should perform reliably across desktop and mobile environments.

## 1. Project objectives

1. Establish immediate professional credibility with senior engineering, operations, asset and risk stakeholders.
2. Explain DeepTsight’s specialist capabilities in clear commercial and operational terms.
3. Demonstrate relevant experience, qualifications, registrations, certifications and publications, subject to verification and approval.
4. Generate qualified enquiries through clear, well-placed calls to action.
5. Create a scalable content foundation for future insights, case studies and service pages.
6. Deliver a secure, accessible, maintainable and high-performing website.

### Recommended navigation

**Home | About | Services | Credentials | Insights | Contact**

### Navigation principles

- Keep top-level navigation concise and predictable.
- Provide a persistent and visually clear contact action.
- Use descriptive page and section labels rather than clever terminology.
- Ensure important content is reachable without relying on hover interactions.
- Include a structured footer with services, contact, legal and LinkedIn links.

# 5. Homepage content architecture

7. **Hero:** a concise value proposition, supporting statement and primary CTA.
8. **Trust strip:** verified credentials, professional registrations, relevant standards knowledge or publication evidence.
9. **Core capabilities:** three or four service pillars with outcome-focused summaries.
10. **Why DeepTsight:** founder-led expertise, practical site experience and integration of engineering, security and reliability.
11. **Problems addressed:** examples of complex upgrades, network segregation, operational risk and lifecycle optimisation.
12. **Delivery approach:** assess, architect, implement and assure, or another approved methodology.
13. **Selected proof:** anonymised project examples, publications or measurable outcomes where disclosure is permitted.
14. **Perth and sector context:** local presence with capability across critical infrastructure and heavy industry.
15. **Final conversion section:** clear invitation to discuss an operational challenge.

### Homepage writing rule

Lead with client problems and outcomes. Use credentials as evidence, not as the opening message. Avoid broad claims such as “world-class”, “leading” or “best-in-class” unless they can be substantiated.

# 7. About and founder narrative

The About page should present a concise, credible progression from early engineering experience in Cochin through site-based work in operated oil and gas assets and power generation, including ATCO Power, to the establishment of DeepTsight in Perth. Dates, employer names, role descriptions and experience claims must be confirmed before publication.

- Explain the practical relevance of the founder’s career, not only the chronology.
- Connect site experience to DeepTsight’s approach to safety, availability, maintainability and implementation.
- Use a professional portrait and authentic project-context imagery where permissions allow.
- Separate personal biography from the company’s client promise and delivery principles.
- Avoid publishing confidential client, site or security information.

# 8. Credentials and proof strategy

Create a structured evidence section for approved and verifiable credentials. Potential categories include:

- Academic qualifications, including MEng details
- Professional registrations, including PEng details
- ISA/IEC 62443-related certifications
- RAMS symposium papers and other publications
- Selected control platforms, engineering tools and standards experience
- Approved project summaries, sectors served and client outcomes

### Verification requirement

Every qualification, registration, certification, publication, client reference, project claim and quantified outcome must be checked for exact wording, current status, permission and expiry where relevant before publication.

# 9. Tone of voice and copywriting guidance

| Principle                    | Use                                                                                                                                             | Avoid                                                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Technical and direct**     | Use precise language recognised by senior engineers and asset owners. Define specialist terminology where a commercial reader may need context. | Dense acronym strings, unexplained jargon and promotional filler.             |
| **Solution-oriented**        | Connect technical work to safety, availability, resilience, lifecycle value and implementation confidence.                                      | Feature lists without explaining why they matter.                             |
| **Professional and assured** | Write with calm confidence, evidence and restraint.                                                                                             | Aggressive selling, exaggerated claims or fear-based cybersecurity messaging. |
| **Specific and credible**    | Use verified examples, clear outputs and approved evidence.                                                                                     | Vague claims such as “innovative solutions” without substance.                |
| **Human and accessible**     | Use short sections, descriptive headings and plain English around technical detail.                                                             | Long walls of text or language that sounds generated or impersonal.           |

# 10. Visual design direction

- Clean, structured layouts that reflect engineering logic without imitating control-system screens.
- Strong grid, deliberate spacing, restrained visual hierarchy and clear content grouping.
- Brand-led use of dark navy or blue-black, steel neutrals and one controlled signal accent, aligned with the final identity.
- High-quality, authentic imagery of industrial assets, control environments, network architecture details and Perth context where relevant.
- Avoid generic people-at-blueprint photography, staged cybersecurity imagery, excessive circuit patterns and visually noisy industrial montages.
- Use diagrams selectively to explain services or architecture concepts, while avoiding disclosure of sensitive operational detail.
- Design for readability and trust before decorative impact.

### Image sourcing and permissions

Specify the source, licence, usage rights and attribution requirements for every image. Client-site images, plant photographs, control-room imagery and network diagrams must be approved for public use and checked for confidential or security-sensitive information.

# 11. User experience requirements

- Responsive design across current desktop, tablet and mobile screen sizes.
- Clear keyboard focus states and fully operable navigation without a mouse.
- Readable typography, sufficient contrast and meaningful link labels.
- Consistent calls to action without interruptive pop-ups.
- Short, logical contact form with clear validation and useful error messages.
- No critical information embedded only within images.
- Breadcrumbs where service or insight content becomes multi-level.
- Search capability only when content volume justifies it.
- Useful 404 and confirmation pages that return visitors to relevant content.

## Accessibility target

**Recommended acceptance target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA for the designed and developed experience.**

Accessibility should be tested through automated checks and manual review, including keyboard navigation, focus order, form labels, contrast, zoom and screen-reader basics.

# 12. Performance, security and privacy requirements

| Area                           | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                    | Acceptance evidence                                                          |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Performance**                | Optimised images and fonts; minimal unnecessary scripts; efficient caching; stable layouts; fast interaction on mobile connections.                                            | Report performance test results for agreed representative pages and devices. |
| **Transport and hosting**      | HTTPS across the site; appropriate certificate management; secure hosting and deployment practices.                                                                            | No mixed content; document hosting, backup and recovery arrangements.        |
| **Application security**       | Supported software and dependencies; secure configuration; least-privilege administration; protected forms; input validation; rate limiting or equivalent anti-abuse controls. | Complete pre-launch security review and remediate agreed findings.           |
| **Administration**             | Multi-factor authentication where supported; named administrator roles; removal of default or unused accounts; auditability of major changes.                                  | Provide an access and ownership register at handover.                        |
| **Privacy**                    | Collect only necessary enquiry information; display a privacy notice; define data destination and retention; use consent controls where required.                              | Map form fields, processors, integrations and retention decisions.           |
| **Monitoring and maintenance** | Availability monitoring, update ownership, backup checks, incident contact and ongoing maintenance responsibilities.                                                           | Provide a practical maintenance and support plan.                            |

### Security positioning principle

Because DeepTsight offers OT cybersecurity services, the website should demonstrate disciplined security and privacy practices. Do not publish architecture, client, vulnerability or operational details that could create unnecessary exposure.

# 13. Search visibility and structured content

- Define one clear search intent and unique title for each core page.
- Use descriptive URLs, page headings and metadata.
- Create useful service copy rather than thin pages built around repeated keywords.
- Add organisation, person, service, article and breadcrumb structured data where accurate and appropriate.
- Provide social-sharing metadata and a controlled preview image.
- Generate XML sitemap and robots directives appropriate to the final environment.
- Plan redirects if replacing an existing site or changing URLs.
- Include local Perth context naturally where it supports the service proposition.

# 14. Analytics and conversion measurement

Measurement should be privacy-conscious and limited to information needed to improve the website and understand qualified engagement.

- Contact form submissions and successful completion rate
- Clicks on email, phone or LinkedIn actions where implemented
- Primary and secondary CTA engagement
- Visits to service and credentials content
- Engagement with approved downloadable material
- Traffic source and search landing-page performance
- Form errors and abandoned steps without collecting sensitive field content

Exact tools, consent requirements, data retention and reporting ownership should be agreed before implementation.

# 15. Content management and governance

- Select a maintainable content platform appropriate to expected publishing needs and support capability.
- Permit authorised editing of core page copy, service content, credentials, insights, metadata and contact details.
- Define content owner, technical owner and approval responsibilities.
- Use draft, review and publish controls where available and appropriate.
- Record renewal dates for domain, hosting, certificates, licences and third-party services.
- Provide a process for updates to credentials, policies, dependencies and staff access.
